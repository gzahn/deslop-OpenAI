# Examples

## Throat-clearing and vague significance

Before

> It is worth noting that the revised process has important implications for how we navigate scheduling challenges moving forward.

After

> The revised process gives instructors two weeks to resolve scheduling conflicts before registration opens.

The revision removes filler and states the supported consequence.

## Binary contrast and dramatic fragments

Before

> The problem is not the form. It is the timing. Three days. That is all departments get.

After

> Departments receive the form only three days before the deadline.

The revision states the constraint directly and removes manufactured emphasis.

## Sentence-level colon and three-part list

Before

> Please bring three items: the signed form, your identification, and the original receipt.

After

> Please bring the signed form and your identification. Bring the original receipt as well.

The revision removes the colon and breaks the three-item series while preserving all requirements.

## Administrative jargon

Before

> Moving forward, we will leverage the new platform to streamline the onboarding ecosystem.

After

> We will use the new platform to shorten onboarding.

The revision replaces business jargon with direct language.

## Preserve literal material

Before

> Submit the file at https://example.edu/forms by 14:30. Use the subject line "Travel request: Smith lab."

After

> Submit the file at https://example.edu/forms by 14:30. Use the subject line "Travel request: Smith lab."

The URLs, timestamp, and required subject line remain exact even though they contain colons.

## Audit-only response

An audit should identify concrete patterns without assigning a score.

- The opening delays the request with two sentences of context that recipients already know.
- The final paragraph repeats the deadline, and one earlier sentence contains a colon followed by a three-item list. Remove the repetition and split the requirements into separate sentences.
