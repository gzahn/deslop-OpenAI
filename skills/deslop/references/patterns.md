# Pattern catalog

Use this catalog when the short checklist in `SKILL.md` is insufficient. Treat these as diagnostic patterns. Context and accumulation matter unless `SKILL.md` marks a rule as absolute.

## Openers and filler

Cut empty setup such as "here's the thing," "it is worth noting," "the reality is," "at its core," and "in today's environment." Begin with the content.

Remove emphasis that adds no information, including "let that sink in," "make no mistake," "full stop," and "this matters because." Explain the consequence directly when the consequence is supported.

Avoid pedagogical prompts such as "let's unpack this," "let's dive in," "think of it as," and "imagine a world where." Use an analogy only when it makes a difficult relationship easier to understand.

## Formulaic structures

Avoid binary pivots built from negation followed by a reveal. State the intended claim directly.

Avoid negative countdowns, dramatic fragments, self-posed questions, repeated sentence openings, and numbered lists disguised as paragraphs.

Do not append shallow participle phrases such as "highlighting the importance" or "underscoring the need." Replace them with a supported consequence or remove them.

Avoid false ranges that use "from X to Y" when the endpoints do not define a meaningful spectrum.

Do not acknowledge a problem only to dismiss it with a canned optimistic transition such as "despite these challenges." Analyze the problem or omit it.

## Word choice

Prefer plain verbs over inflated substitutes. "Is" is often better than "serves as," "stands as," or "represents."

Use words such as "delve," "tapestry," "landscape," "robust," "leverage," "nuanced," and "framework" only when they are the most precise terms in context.

Delete adverbs that provide empty emphasis. Keep adverbs that carry necessary meaning.

Avoid invented labels such as "the coordination paradox" unless the writer defines and needs the term.

Avoid vague authority claims. Name the source when the source is available. Retain appropriate uncertainty when evidence is incomplete.

## Agency and specificity

Name the person or group responsible when that information is known and relevant. Do not force human agency into descriptions of processes that genuinely lack a single actor.

Replace statements such as "the implications are significant" with the supported implication. Do not fabricate an implication merely to eliminate vagueness.

Scale claims to the evidence. Routine administrative changes rarely reshape a field or define an era.

## Rhythm and composition

Vary sentence length. Avoid a run of equally sized sentences or stacked fragments designed to sound forceful.

State each point once, support it, and move on. Remove conclusions that repeat the preceding paragraph and metaphors repeated after they have done their work.

Use one well-explained example instead of a rapid sequence of names or historical analogies.

Avoid bold-first bullets as a default formatting habit. Use headings, ordinary bullets, prose, or a table according to the information structure.

## Absolute checks

Finished prose must contain no newly written em dash, sentence-level colon, or three-item series. Preserve these marks when they occur inside literal material that must remain exact.
